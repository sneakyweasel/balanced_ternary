# k-abelian residual signatures of automatic sequences

Status: **ARCHIVED**

Whether the laboratory’s residual/signature machinery isolates a finite
state that explains known \(b\)-regular \(k\)-abelian complexity, or
whether that state is the classical \(k\)-block-coding construction.
The general regularity conjecture is not the Phase-0 target.

## Problem

For a \(b\)-automatic sequence \(x\), can \(k\)-abelian equivalence
classes of factors be represented by a finite residual state whose
\(b\)-kernel induces the \(b\)-regular complexity sequence
\(\rho_k^{\mathrm{ab}}(x,n)\)? The question is the mechanism, not a
computation of known complexity tables.

## Exact statement

Two finite words \(u,v\) are \(k\)-abelian equivalent when they have
the same number of overlapping occurrences of every factor of length
at most \(k\) (Karhumäki–Saarela–Zamboni). The complexity
\(\rho_k^{\mathrm{ab}}(x,n)\) counts those classes among length-\(n\)
factors of \(x\).

Keep three objects distinct:

1. the raw factor \(u\);
2. the signature \(\Sigma_k(u)=(|u|_w)_{|w|\le k}\);
3. a behavioural state sufficient to determine future classes.

The Phase-0 question is whether \(\Sigma_k\) compresses to a finite
transition system when \(x\) is automatic, other than the known
\(k\)-block coding of \(x\).

Candidate states, tested rather than assumed:

- raw \(\Sigma_k\);
- the KSZ triple \((\mathrm{pref}_{k-1},\mathrm{suff}_{k-1},\psi_k)\);
- the naive pair \((\text{DFAO state at the start},\mathrm{suff}_{k-1})\);
- relative KSZ triples (componentwise shift by the length-\(n\) minimum).

The general conjecture

\[
x\text{ is }\ell\text{-automatic}
\implies
\rho_k^{\mathrm{ab}}(x,n)\text{ is }\ell\text{-regular}
\]

is recorded as `kabelian_regularity_automatic` and is not attacked.

## Current literature

- `karhumaki-saarela-zamboni-2013-k-abelian`: definition of
  \(k\)-abelian equivalence and complexity; for \(|u|\ge k-1\) the
  relation is equal length-\((k-1)\) prefixes and suffixes together
  with equal length-\(k\) factor counts. `KNOWN`.
- `parreau-rigo-rowland-vandomme-2015-2-regular`: 2-abelian complexity
  of Thue–Morse and period-doubling is 2-regular, by \(2\)-block
  coding followed by abelian complexity of the block sequence. Poses
  the general regularity conjecture. `KNOWN`.
- `greinecker-2015-tm-2-abelian`: independent 2-regularity proof for
  Thue–Morse via recurrences. `KNOWN`.
- `chen-lu-wu-2017-cantor-k-abelian`: for every \(k\ge 1\), the
  \(k\)-abelian complexity of the Cantor sequence is 3-regular.
  `KNOWN`.
- `shallit-2020-abelian-synchronization`: if abelian complexity is
  bounded and prefix Parikh vectors are synchronized, Walnut builds a
  DFAO for \(\rho^{\mathrm{ab}}\). `KNOWN`.
- `couvreur-et-al-2025-pisot-k-abelian`: two Walnut constructions
  (uniform factor-balancedness; ultimately Pisot substitutions, again
  via \(k\)-block coding). The general conjecture is still listed as
  unresolved. `KNOWN`.
- `allouche-shallit-2003-automatic-sequences`: \(b\)-automatic iff
  finite \(b\)-kernel; \(b\)-regular iff the \(\mathbb{Z}\)-module
  generated by the kernel is finitely generated. `KNOWN`.

No source located in the gate states a laboratory residual that is
not the KSZ triple / block coding. New notation for that triple is
not evidence of novelty.

## Balanced-ternary formulation

Phase 0 writes the three benchmarks in their classical alphabets
\(\{0,1\}\), with Cantor generated by unsigned base-3 digits. A
balanced-trit address of the same 3-automatic sequence is a
coordinate change of the input to the existing DFAO and was not
opened. If the unsigned construction is already `KNOWN`, the
balanced comparison is a `REPARAMETERIZATION`.

## Why BT may be relevant

The laboratory already minimizes DFAs and records residual
congruence failures. Base-3 automatic sequences (Cantor) are a
natural test. Relevance is not a novelty claim: nothing in the
census used a balanced digit, and no BT-specific state bound appeared.

## Candidate operations / invariants

- \(\Sigma_k\) with overlapping counts — **KNOWN** (KSZ definition).
- Local update \(\Sigma_k(ua)=F_k(\Sigma_k(u),\mathrm{suff}_{k-1}(u),a)\)
  — **KNOWN** from the definition; **COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED** as an
  exact identity on every benchmark factor in the census.
- KSZ triple \((\mathrm{pref}_{k-1},\mathrm{suff}_{k-1},\psi_k)\) —
  **KNOWN** / **REPARAMETERIZATION** of \(k\)-block coding plus
  borders (Parreau–Rigo–Rowland–Vandomme; Couvreur et al. §4.2).
- Naive pair \((\text{DFAO state},\mathrm{suff}_{k-1})\) — finite of
  size at most \(N\cdot q^{k-1}\); **COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED** to be
  strictly coarser than the class for \(k\ge 2\) on every row with
  \(n\ge 8\).
- Raw \(\Sigma_k\) as a state — unbounded: the largest coordinate
  grows with \(n\). **COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED**.
- Sliding-window Rauzy quotient of KSZ classes — **not** a
  congruence. **COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED** (positive conflict counts
  for every \(n\ge 8\)).
- Growing-factor extension of KSZ classes — a congruence: zero
  conflicts on every census row. **COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED**. The
  state still carries an unbounded Parikh vector.
- Empirical \(b\)-kernel of a length-24 prefix of \(\rho\) —
  **OBSERVATION** only. Not a regularity theorem.

## Counterexamples

1. **Raw \(\Sigma_k\) is not a finite state.** On Thue–Morse with
   \(k=2\), the largest signature coordinate is \(1,5,9,13\) at
   \(n=1,8,16,24\). Identifying classes by raw counts yields an
   infinite transition system as \(n\) grows.

2. **DFAO state plus suffix does not determine the class.** For
   Thue–Morse, \(k=2\), \(n=24\), there are 4 naive pairs and 12
   classes. The bound \(N q^{k-1}\) is finite and insufficient.

3. **KSZ classes are not a sliding-window congruence.** Sliding a
   length-\(n\) window removes a length-\(k\) block that the
   \((k-1)\)-prefix does not determine. Thue–Morse \(k=1\), \(n=8\)
   already has 1360 conflicting Rauzy transitions. This is the same
   kind of obstruction as the closed Černý gate (`≡_r` is not a
   transition congruence), for a different relation.

4. **A finite catalogue of relative KSZ shapes is not the
   regularity mechanism.** Period-doubling \(k=2\) relative-union
   sizes along \(n=1,2,4,8,12,16,24\) are
   \(2,5,10,16,24,24,33\). Cantor \(k=1\) gives \(2,2,5,9,14,14,21\).
   The class-side residual grows in the census whenever
   \(\rho_k^{\mathrm{ab}}\) is unbounded. \(b\)-regularity is
   finiteness of a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-module of *length-indexed*
   subsequences, a different object.

These witnesses are regression-tested in
`tests/research/kabelian_complexity/test_triage.py`.

## Results

### What was learned

- The local growing-factor update is exact: \(\Sigma_k(ua)\) is
  determined by \(\Sigma_k(u)\), the length-\((k-1)\) suffix, and
  \(a\). Extension of KSZ classes is a congruence.
- The KSZ triple *is* the class. It is the \(k\)-block Parikh vector
  plus borders, which is the 2015 block-coding construction.
- Raw signatures and relative class catalogues grow with \(n\) on
  the unbounded-\(\rho\) examples. A finite \(n\)-independent class
  residual is therefore not the source of \(b\)-regularity.
- The naive finite pair (DFAO state, suffix) is strictly coarser
  than the class for \(k\ge 2\).
- Sliding-window equivalence of KSZ classes is not a transition
  congruence. Growing-factor equivalence is.
- Published prefixes and small-\(n\) values recover: Thue–Morse
  abelian complexity \(2,3,2,3,\ldots\) through \(n=24\); 2-abelian
  complexity unbounded; Cantor \(c=101000101\cdots\). These are
  `KNOWN` theorems plus `COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED` tables.
- Empirical kernel prefix-counts are finite in the table
  (Thue–Morse \(k=2\) gives 6 distinct 4-term kernel prefixes).
  That is an `OBSERVATION`, not a regularity proof.

### Strongest theorem

None promoted. Every exact identity used in the gate is `KNOWN`
(KSZ lemma, local count update, block coding) or a finite check of
those identities.

### Strongest refutation

The hypothesis that a finite residual of \(k\)-abelian *factor
classes* induces \(b\)-regular complexity fails: whenever
\(\rho_k^{\mathrm{ab}}\) is unbounded, the class residual is
unbounded, while \(b\)-regularity concerns the kernel of the
*counting sequence* \(\rho(n)\). The naive finite pair (DFAO,
suffix) does not recover the class.

### Census (COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED, \(n\le 24\))

Thue–Morse, \(k=1\): \(\rho=(2,3,2,3,\ldots,2,3)\). Matches the
known odd/even abelian complexity. Naive states \(2\); classes \(2\)
or \(3\).

Thue–Morse, \(k=2\):
\(\rho=(2,4,6,8,6,8,10,8,6,8,8,10,10,10,8,8,6,8,10,10,8,10,12,12)\).
At \(n=24\): 76 factors, 12 classes, 4 naive states, 12 Rauzy
vertices, 1228 sliding conflicts, 0 extension conflicts.

Period-doubling, \(k=2\), \(n=24\): 40 factors, 9 classes, 3 naive
states.

Cantor, \(k=1,2,3\) at \(n=24\): \(\rho=7,19,29\); naive states
\(2,3,5\). \(k=3\) was cheap and stayed inside the gate.

Empirical kernel 4-term prefix counts (OBSERVATION): TM \(1,2\) give
\(3,6\); period-doubling \(4,7\); Cantor \(3,4,4\).

### Gate verdict

`CLOSE`

The factor-side residual is the known block-coding triple. It is a
growing-factor congruence and not a sliding congruence. It is
finite per \(n\) and infinite as \(n\) varies precisely when
\(\rho\) is unbounded. That cannot be the mechanism of
\(b\)-regularity. Walnut and 2015–2025 block-coding already supply
the length-side construction under extra hypotheses. No distinct
laboratory residual survived the gate.

## Decision

`CLOSE — REPARAMETERIZATION`. The primary gate was a finite residual
of \(k\)-abelian classes distinct from \(k\)-block coding. The class
is exactly the KSZ triple, which is that construction. Raw
signatures are unbounded. The only finite naive state is strictly
too coarse. Sliding KSZ classes do not form a congruence.
Growing-factor KSZ classes do, and still carry unbounded Parikh
data. Every surviving mathematical statement is `KNOWN` or
`REPARAMETERIZATION`. No CLI, visualization, Lean, ledger row, or
numbered milestone is justified.

Best next question: none; the branch is not promoted.
