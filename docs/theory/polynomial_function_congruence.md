# Polynomial function congruence modulo \(3^k\)

Master record for Milestone 18. The section operator remains a
**(3)-section / Cartier-style reparameterization**. This document is
about the kernel of

\[
\mathbb Z[x]\longrightarrow\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z)

and its meaning for residual Myhill–Nerode classes. It does **not**
give a formula for \(M_k(x^d)\).

## 1. Three equalities

Keep these separate.

### Polynomial equality

\[
f=g\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[x].
\]

### Pointwise equality on \(\mathbb Z\)

\[
f(n)=g(n)\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

For ordinary integer polynomials these two coincide: a nonzero
\(h\in\mathbb Z[x]\) has only finitely many roots unless it is the zero
polynomial.

### Function equality modulo \(3^k\)

\[
f(n)\equiv g(n)\pmod{3^k}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

This is the new object. Distinct ordinary polynomials **can** agree as
functions at a finite modulus. They cannot agree at every finite
horizon: if \(f\equiv_k g\) for all \(k\), then \(f=g\) in \(\mathbb Z[x]\).
Milestone 17 already recorded that finite merges are delayed
distinctions, not infinite equivalences.

## 2. Definition and Myhill–Nerode bridge

Write \(h=f-g\). Residual equivalence at horizon \(k\) means that every
trit word of length \(k\) produces the same output word.

**EXACT — LEAN VERIFIED** (`equivK_iff_functionCongr`):

\[
\boxed{
f\equiv_k g
\iff
\forall n\in\mathbb Z,\quad
3^k\mid(f(n)-g(n)).
}
\]

Reason:

- Prefix locality says \(f\equiv_k g\) iff the length-\(k\) integer jets
  of \(f(n_w)\) and \(g(n_w)\) agree for every packed prefix \(n_w\).
- Packed prefixes of length \(k\) are a complete residue system
  modulo \(3^k\).
- Every \(h\in\mathbb Z[x]\) preserves congruences:
  \(a\equiv b\pmod{3^k}\) implies \(h(a)\equiv h(b)\pmod{3^k}\).

So “all relevant integers” are all integers. The automata problem for
ordinary polynomial residuals is an arithmetic classification problem.

Then \(M_k(f)\) is the number of distinct polynomial-function classes
generated by the residual set \(U_k=\{f_w:|w|<k\}\).

## 3. The kernel \(I_k\)

\[
I_k
=
\{h\in\mathbb Z[x]:
h(n)\equiv 0\pmod{3^k}\ \forall n\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]

Coefficientwise membership \(3^k\mathbb Z[x]\subseteq I_k\) is immediate
and **not** an equality for \(\deg\ge 3\).

Write \(h\) in the Newton / binomial basis

\[
h(x)=\sum_{j\ge 0}a_j\binom{x}{j},
\qquad
a_j=\Delta^j h(0)\in\mathbb Z.
\]

This expansion is unique, and \(\binom{n}{j}\in\mathbb Z\) for every
integer \(n\). Forward differences of multiples of \(3^k\) remain
multiples of \(3^k\). Therefore:

**EXACT — LEAN VERIFIED** (`vanishesMod_iff_newtonKernel`; Newton
interpolation; REPARAMETERIZATION of the classical theory of
polynomial functions on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\)):

\[
\boxed{
h\in I_k
\iff
3^k\mid a_j
\quad\text{for every }j.
}
\]

The monomial form \(h=\sum c_j x^j\) is related by Stirling numbers of
the second kind:

\[
a_j=j!\sum_i c_i\,S(i,j).
\]

The factor \(j!\) is why degree \(\ge 3\) is different: \(v_3(3!)=1\).

The images of \(\{\binom{x}{j}:j\ge 0\}\) remain linearly independent
as functions modulo \(3^k\), because evaluation at \(0,1,2,\ldots\) is
triangular with ones on the diagonal. So the binomial residues
parameterize functions injectively.

## 4. Canonical finite invariant

For any \(f\in\mathbb Z[x]\),

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_k(f)
=
\bigl(\Delta^j f(0)\bmod 3^k\bigr)_{j\ge 0}.
}
\]

Only \(j\le\deg f\) can be nonzero. After zero-padding,

\[
\boxed{
f\equiv_k g
\iff
\Phi_k(f)=\Phi_k(g).
}
\]

**EXACT — LEAN VERIFIED** (`equivK_iff_newtonCoeff`) for every
degree. Degree \(\le 2\) and degree \(3\) remain the explicit
coefficient forms `vanishesMod_quad_iff` and `vanishesMod_cubic_iff`.
**COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED** for mixed degrees in
`test_poly_congruence.py`.

Equivalent coordinates, all complete for a fixed degree bound \(d\):

| coordinate | data modulo \(3^k\) |
|---|---|
| Newton / binomial | \(\Delta^j f(0)\), \(j=0,\ldots,d\) |
| values on \(\{0,1,\ldots,d\}\) | Newton interpolation is unitriangular over \(\mathbb Z\) |
| values on a complete residue system | tautological, not minimal |
| monomial coefficients | **not** injective for \(d\ge 3\) |

Balanced probes \(\{-\lfloor d/2\rfloor,\ldots,\lceil d/2\rceil\}\) also
determine the function, but the Newton window \(\{0,\ldots,d\}\) is the
one that makes the inverse integer and triangular.

This is the correct replacement of the quadratic triple
\((A,B,C)\bmod 3^k\).

## 5. Degree \(\le 2\) (baseline)

\[
h(x)=Ax^2+Bx+C.
\]

Then

\[
h(x)=C\binom{x}{0}+(A+B)\binom{x}{1}+2A\binom{x}{2}.
\]

So \(h\in I_k\) iff \(3^k\mid C\), \(3^k\mid(A+B)\), and \(3^k\mid 2A\).
Since \(2\) is a unit modulo \(3^k\),

\[
h\in I_k
\iff
3^k\mid A,\quad
3^k\mid B,\quad
3^k\mid C.
\]

The probes \(0,1,-1\) recover the same three residues: \(h(0)=C\),
\(h(1)+h(-1)=2A+2C\), \(h(1)-h(-1)=2B\).

**EXACT — LEAN VERIFIED** (`vanishesMod_quad_iff`, `equivK_quad`).
Preserved from Milestone 17; not duplicated as a second quadratic
machine.

## 6. Degree 3: first higher-degree obstruction

\[
h(x)=Ax^3+Bx^2+Cx+D.
\]

The Newton identity used in Lean is the integer ring calculation

\[
h(x)=D+(A+B+C)x+(3A+B)x(x-1)+Ax(x-1)(x-2),
\]

together with \(6\mid x(x-1)(x-2)\). Therefore:

**EXACT — LEAN VERIFIED** (`vanishesMod_cubic_iff`):

\[
h\in I_k
\iff
3^k\mid D,\quad
3^k\mid(A+B+C),\quad
3^k\mid(3A+B),\quad
3^k\mid 6A.
\]

The last condition is \(v_3(A)\ge k-1\) for \(k\ge 1\), **not**
\(3^k\mid A\).

### First invisible polynomial

**EXACT — LEAN VERIFIED** (`X_pow_three_sub_X_vanishes_one`,
`not_three_dvd_coeff_X_pow_three_sub_X`):

\[
x^3-x=x(x-1)(x+1)\in I_1\setminus 3\mathbb Z[x].
\]

Degree \(3\). Coefficients \((0,-1,0,1)\). Horizon \(k=1\).
Coefficient valuations: \(\min v_3(c_j)=0\). Newton valuations:
\(a=(0,0,6,6)\), \(\min v_3(a_j)=1\). Factorization: three consecutive
integers. Generated by the identity \(x^3\equiv x\pmod 3\).

**COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED** (`vanishing-poly 3 --k 1`): this is the
smallest invisible polynomial of degree \(\le 3\) at \(k=1\). At
general \(k\ge 1\) the smallest invisible cubic is
\(3^{k-1}(x^3-x)\). Degree \(\le 2\) has **no** invisible polynomials.

Coefficientwise divisibility is therefore **REFUTED** as a necessary
condition for \(\deg\ge 3\).

## 7. Valuation

For \(h=\sum c_j x^j=\sum a_j\binom{x}{j}\),

\[
\min_j v_3(a_j)
\ge
\min_j v_3(c_j),
\]

because each \(a_j\) is a \(\mathbb Z\)-linear combination of the
\(c_i\). Cancellations can make the inequality strict:
\(x^3-x\) has monomial minimum \(0\) and Newton minimum \(1\).

The function-vanishing threshold is the **Newton** minimum, not the
coefficientwise minimum.

A monomial upper bound is available from the leading term
\(a_d=d!\,\mathrm{lc}(h)\):

\[
\min_j v_3(a_j)
\le
v_3(d!)+v_3(\mathrm{lc}(h)).
\]

Do **not** fit a heuristic such as “\(\tau=1+\min v_3(c_j)\)”. That
formula is **REFUTED** by \(x^3-x\) (it predicts distinction at
horizon \(1\), but \(x^3\equiv_1 x\)).

## 8. Delayed distinction

For distinct ordinary polynomials define

\[
\tau(f,g)
=
\min\{k:f\not\equiv_k g\}.
\]

**EXACT — HUMAN PROOF.** If \(h=f-g\neq 0\) and
\(v=\min_j v_3(\Delta^j h(0))\), then \(f\equiv_k g\) iff \(k\le v\), so

\[
\boxed{
\tau(f,g)=1+\min_j v_3\bigl(\Delta^j(f-g)(0)\bigr).
}
\]

In particular \(\tau\) is finite. Combined with the leading-term bound,

\[
\tau(f,g)
\le
1+v_3(d!)+v_3(\mathrm{lc}(f-g)).
\]

**COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED** on the first residual merges of \(x^3\)
and \(x^4\):

| pair | \(\min v_3(c)\) | \(\min v_3(a)\) | \(\tau\) | leading bound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(x^3-x\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(2\) | \(2\) |
| \(x^3\) residuals \((-1)\) vs \((1)\) | \(2\) | \(2\) | \(3\) | \(3\) |
| \(x^4\) residuals \((0)\) vs \((0,0)\) | \(3\) | \(3\) | \(4\) | \(5\) |
| \(x^4\) residuals \((0,-1)\) vs \((0,1)\) | \(3\) | \(3\) | \(4\) | \(7\) |
| \(x^4\) residuals \((0,0)\) vs \((0,0,0)\) at \(k=4\) | \(6\) | \(6\) | \(7\) | — |

Delayed distinction can last several extra horizons. The duration is
exactly the Newton valuation, not a one-step phenomenon.

## 10. The \(x^4\) first merge

\[
f_{(0)}=27x^4,
\qquad
f_{(0,0)}=729x^4,
\qquad
f_{(0)}-f_{(0,0)}=-702x^4.
\]

Here \(702=2\cdot 3^3\cdot 13\), so the monomial valuation is already
\(3\). The Newton profile is
\((0,-702,-9828,-25272,-16848)\) with minimum valuation \(3\). Hence
\(\equiv_3\) and not \(\equiv_4\), i.e. \(\tau=4\). The leading-term
bound \(1+v_3(24)+3=5\) is correct but not sharp.

The odd/even pair \((0,-1)\) vs \((0,1)\) has difference
\(-216x-1944x^3\) and the same Newton minimum \(3\), so the same
\(\tau=4\).

**EXACT — LEAN VERIFIED** for the zero-prefix pair

`x4_first_merge_equiv_three`, `x4_first_merge_not_equiv_four`).
**COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED** for the odd/even pair.

## 11. Dimension of degree-\(\le d\) functions

The binomial residues \((a_0,\ldots,a_d)\bmod 3^k\) are free
coordinates. Therefore the module of polynomial functions of degree
at most \(d\) over \(\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z\) is free of rank \(d+1\):

\[
\bigl|\{\text{functions induced by }\deg\le d\}\bigr|
=
(3^k)^{d+1}.
\]

**EXACT — HUMAN PROOF** (unitriangular evaluation at \(0,\ldots,d\)).
The monomial coefficient module \(\mathbb Z^{d+1}\) maps onto this
space with a nontrivial kernel for \(d\ge 3\). Do not treat monomial
residues as a free basis of functions.

This is a statement about all of \(\mathbb Z[x]_{\le d}\), not about
the residual orbit of a single \(x^d\). It does **not** compute
\(M_k(x^d)\).
