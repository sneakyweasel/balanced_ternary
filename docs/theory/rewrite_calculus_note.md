# An Operator-Oriented Unary Calculus for Balanced Ternary and a Locality Obstruction for Addition

**Status:** prior-art-corrected publication draft.

## Abstract

Balanced ternary and signed-digit arithmetic have long-standing
theories of digit append, carry management, finite-state normalization,
and arithmetic rewriting. In particular, Contejean, Marché, and
Rabehasaina (CMR97) gave convergent rewrite systems for natural,
integral, and rational arithmetic using balanced-ternary digit append.
Against that background, we study the narrower operator language
\[
I_a(x)=a+3x,\qquad
D(n)=\frac{n-\operatorname{lsd}(n)}3,\qquad
S(n)=3n,\qquad N(n)=-n .
\]
Here \(I_a\) is the standard balanced-ternary digit-append map and
\(D\) removes the least-significant digit. We formulate a one-hole
unary term language generated by \(\{D,I_a,S,N\}\) and formally verify
in Lean that its finite rewrite system terminates and is confluent,
that rewriting preserves integer-function semantics, and that distinct
irreducible terms denote distinct maps \(\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\).

We also isolate an exact state boundary at addition. Call a binary output
\(H\) \(D\)-local if
\[
H(x,y)=G(D(x),D(y))
\]
for some \(G:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\). We prove that the next-state
output \(H(x,y)=D(x+y)\) is not \(D\)-local. The minimal witness is
\(D(0)=D(1)=0\), while \(D(0+0)=0\) and \(D(1+1)=1\). The exact
identity
\[
D(x+y)=D(x)+D(y)+
\operatorname{carry}(\operatorname{lsd}(x),\operatorname{lsd}(y))
\]
identifies the discarded information. A named extension that pushes
\(S\) through addition is not locally confluent at \(D\circ S\).
These results concern the stated open unary grammar and factorization
notion. They do not claim priority for balanced-ternary arithmetic
rewriting, finite-state carry, or parallel signed-digit addition, and
they do not classify arbitrary rewrite systems for arithmetic.

## 1. Introduction

Every integer has a unique balanced-ternary expansion
\[
n=\sum_{i\ge0}a_i3^i,\qquad a_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}.
\]
This representation and its arithmetic are classical. Avižienis
established the broad signed-digit principle that redundancy limits
carry propagation and enables parallel arithmetic. Heuberger and
Prodinger later analyzed carry propagation with automata in signed-digit
systems, explicitly including balanced ternary. Frougny, Pelantová, and
Svobodová developed general local and parallel addition algorithms and
studied minimal supporting alphabets; subsequent work treated fixed
blocks.

Arithmetic rewriting is also direct prior art. CMR97 represents an
integer by balanced-ternary postfix digit append \(x :_t a\), with
\(\llbracket x :_t a\rrbracket=3\llbracket x\rrbracket+a\), and gives
terminating and confluent arithmetic rewrite systems modulo
associativity and commutativity of addition and multiplication.
Therefore \(I_a(x)=3x+a\) in this paper is not a new section
construction: it is the same integer map in operator notation. Likewise,
\(D\) is the standard quotient that removes the least-significant
balanced digit.

The remaining question is narrower:

> Do the natural balanced-ternary unary operators form a canonical
> rewrite calculus, and what exact obstruction appears when addition
> is required to use the same local state?

The answer has four parts.

1. The unary language generated by \(\{D,I_a,S,N\}\) has a canonical
   presentation on its specified one-hole syntax, including semantic
   injectivity of irreducible open terms.
2. The output \(D(x+y)\) does not factor through
   \((D(x),D(y))\).
3. The balanced carry identity identifies the missing
   least-significant-trit information.
4. The natural carry-free push-in extension has a non-joining critical
   peak at \(D\circ S\).

The first result is an operator-oriented formalization using standard
rewriting theory. The second is the central state-factorization theorem.
The third is known carry arithmetic in the present notation, and the
fourth shows how that boundary appears in one named syntactic extension.
The Lean development verifies the exact grammar, relation, and theorem
statements; no claim is made that it is the first mechanization of
signed-digit arithmetic.

No claim is made that addition lacks a finite presentation.
Coefficient-word normalization is an explicit complete treatment of
integer sums, but it retains normalization state absent from the unary
tree calculus.

## 2. Balanced-ternary unary language

Let \(\operatorname{lsd}(n)\in\{-1,0,+1\}\) be the balanced residue of
\(n\) modulo \(3\). Define
\[
D(n)=\frac{n-\operatorname{lsd}(n)}3,\qquad
I_a(n)=a+3n,\qquad
S(n)=3n,\qquad
N(n)=-n .
\]
Here \(a\in\{-1,0,+1\}\), and \(I_0=S\) as integer maps.

The formal term grammar is
```text
t ::= var | D(t) | I-(t) | I0(t) | I+(t) | S(t) | N(t).
```
It is a one-hole unary grammar. It contains neither `Add` nor `Mul`,
and it is not the operator-word string language used elsewhere in the
project.

Evaluation assigns an integer function to each term:
\[
\llbracket-\rrbracket:\operatorname{OpFrag}\to(\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z).
\]
For example,
\[
\llbracket D(t)\rrbracket(n)=D(\llbracket t\rrbracket(n)),\qquad
\llbracket I_a(t)\rrbracket(n)=I_a(\llbracket t\rrbracket(n)).
\]
Semantic equality below means equality as functions on every integer,
not agreement at one input.

## 3. Canonical rewrite calculus

The rewrite relation is generated by the following root rules and
closed under every unary context:
```text
I0(t)      → S(t)
D(I-(t))   → t
D(I0(t))   → t
D(I+(t))   → t
D(S(t))    → t
N(N(t))    → t
N(S(t))    → S(N(t))
N(I0(t))   → S(N(t))
N(I-(t))   → I+(N(t))
N(I+(t))   → I-(N(t))
N(D(t))    → D(N(t)).
```
Every rule is an exact identity of integer functions.

The final orientation is necessary for semantic canonicity. Without
\(N(D(t))\to D(N(t))\), the terms \(N(D(\mathrm{var}))\) and
\(D(N(\mathrm{var}))\) are distinct irreducibles with identical
integer semantics.

### 3.1 Canonical-form theorem

**Theorem 1 (canonical unary calculus).** On `OpFrag`:

1. every rewrite sequence terminates;
2. the relation is locally confluent and hence confluent;
3. every term has a unique syntactic normal form;
4. rewriting preserves evaluation on \(\mathbb Z\);
5. two irreducible terms with equal evaluation on every integer are
   syntactically equal.

Consequently, every term has a unique normal descendant, and that
descendant is the unique irreducible representative of its integer
function within this grammar.

The Lean façade theorem is `unary_complete_canonical_form`.

### 3.2 Proof structure

Termination uses the lexicographic rank
\[
(\#I_0,\ \text{\(N\)-inversions},\ \text{term size}).
\]
An \(N\)-inversion is a pair consisting of an \(N\)-node and a
pushable descendant among \(D,I_-,I_0,I_+,S\). Cancellation decreases
size, \(I_0\to S\) decreases the first coordinate, and moving \(N\)
inward decreases the second.

The finite family of root and root-versus-inner critical pairs joins.
Termination and local confluence give confluence by Newman's lemma.
Semantic injectivity of irreducibles then follows from the normal-form
grammar and uniqueness of balanced-ternary words.

The general proof method is standard. The project-specific object is
the exact operator fragment, including the necessary orientation of
\(N\) past \(D\).

## 4. \(D\)-locality and the main theorem

### 4.1 Definition

A binary integer output \(H:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\) is
\(D\)-local if there exists \(G:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\) such that
\[
\forall x,y\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
H(x,y)=G(D(x),D(y)).
\]
Thus \(H\) is \(D\)-local exactly when it factors through the pair of
operand \(D\)-states. This is the only locality notion used here.

For addition, the relevant output is not \(x+y\) itself but its next
state
\[
H_{\mathrm{add}}(x,y)=D(x+y).
\]

### 4.2 Exact non-locality

**Theorem 2 (addition next-state non-locality).**
\[
\boxed{
\neg\exists G:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\quad
\forall x,y,\quad
D(x+y)=G(D(x),D(y)).
}
\]

**Proof.** The inputs \(0\) and \(1\) have the same \(D\)-state:
\[
D(0)=D(1)=0.
\]
Therefore the pairs \((0,0)\) and \((1,1)\) present the same argument
\((0,0)\) to any proposed \(G\). But
\[
D(0+0)=0,\qquad D(1+1)=D(2)=1.
\]
No such \(G\) exists. \(\square\)

The Lean theorem is `add_not_DLocal`. Its predicate `DLocal` is the
factorization definition above, applied to
`fun x y => DZ (x + y)`.

The result is stronger than the failure of the single formula
\(D(x+y)=D(x)+D(y)\): it rules out every function of
\((D(x),D(y))\) alone.

## 5. Addition and the missing carry

Balanced-ternary addition satisfies the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
D(x+y)=D(x)+D(y)+
\operatorname{carry}(\operatorname{lsd}(x),\operatorname{lsd}(y)).
}
\]
In Lean, the carry is the second component of
`addDigit (lsdZ x) (lsdZ y)`. It belongs to
\(\{-1,0,+1\}\).

The identity explains Theorem 2. The states \(D(x)\) and \(D(y)\)
retain the higher parts of the operands after their least-significant
trits have been removed. Those removed trits determine whether their
sum leaves the digit set and produces a carry into the next position.
Hence the carry is not an implementation accident: it is precisely the
information absent from \((D(x),D(y))\).

At the minimal witness,
\[
D(1+1)=D(2)=1
\ne 0=D(1)+D(1).
\]
The Lean theorem `carry_of_add` is a rearrangement of the existing
exact theorem `D_add`.

## 6. Natural carry-free extension and critical peak

Consider the syntax
```text
t ::= X | Y | D(t) | S(t) | Add(t,t)
```
with the named relation generated by
```text
D(S(t))          → t
S(Add(t,u))      → Add(S(t),S(u))
```
and congruence. There is deliberately no rule pushing \(D\) through
`Add`, because the carry-free candidate is unsound.

The term
\[
D(S(\operatorname{Add}(X,Y)))
\]
has two one-step descendants:
\[
\operatorname{Add}(X,Y)
\]
by \(D(S(t))\to t\), and
\[
D(\operatorname{Add}(S(X),S(Y)))
\]
by pushing \(S\) through `Add` inside \(D\). Both descendants are
irreducible in this named system, and they are syntactically distinct.
Nevertheless, both evaluate to \(x+y\).

**Theorem 3 (named push-in obstruction).** The named carry-free
push-in relation is not locally confluent.

The Lean theorems are `pushIn_not_locally_confluent` and
`pushIn_peak_semantic`.

This is a concrete obstruction to one natural extension. It is not a
classification of every rewrite system containing addition, and it is
not used to prove Theorem 2. Rather, it shows the same locality
boundary appearing as a critical peak.

## 7. Restricted maximality question

Theorem 2 immediately excludes any exact first-digit implementation
whose output \(D(x+y)\) is required to be a function only of
\((D(x),D(y))\). That is the precise locality boundary.

A stronger theorem about a class of complete tree rewrite systems
would additionally require an independent syntactic definition of
“carry-free” and a proof that completeness in that class forces
\(D\)-locality. Defining the class by assuming \(D\)-locality, or by
assuming that the two descendants in §6 remain irreducible, would only
restate the conclusion. No such universal maximality statement is
claimed here.

The broader project-level observation that the examined Add systems
either remain incomplete or introduce constants, carry state, or
coefficient normalization is retained as a human research record, not
as a theorem of this note.

## 8. Related Work and Positioning

### 8.1 Balanced-ternary and signed-digit arithmetic

Balanced ternary is the radix-\(3\) signed-digit system with alphabet
\(\{-1,0,1\}\). Unique finite representation of integers, digitwise
negation, and the append operation \(x\mapsto3x+a\) are established
background. Avižienis's 1961 work is the classical source for redundant
signed-digit arithmetic: redundancy bounds carry propagation and
supports parallel addition and subtraction. The broad arithmetic
principle predates this paper by decades.

Our alphabet is nonredundant as an integer representation, but addition
temporarily leaves it: \(1+1=2\) requires a carry and a returned digit
\(-1\). The identity in §5 expresses that familiar phenomenon through
\(D\) and \(\operatorname{lsd}\). It is not presented as a new carry
algorithm.

### 8.2 Rewrite systems for arithmetic

CMR97 is the central comparison. It gives algebraic presentations of
naturals, integers, and rationals by convergent rewrite systems. Its
integer representation uses balanced-ternary postfix digit append with
digits \(-1,0,1\):
\[
\llbracket x :_t a\rrbracket=3\llbracket x\rrbracket+a.
\]
Its arithmetic system includes addition and multiplication and is
terminating and confluent modulo their associativity and commutativity.
Thus CMR97 already provides balanced-ternary arithmetic rewriting and
predates this work. Walters and Zantema independently provide broader
arbitrary-radix digit-application and integer-arithmetic TRSs; Bergstra
and Ponse later place CMR97 within that digit-append tradition.

The present system solves a different, smaller rewrite problem: open
one-hole unary terms over \(\{D,I_a,S,N\}\), with equality interpreted
as equality of functions \(\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\). It contains no
arithmetic constructor and uses no AC matching. Its strongest canonical
form statement includes semantic injectivity of irreducible open terms.
That difference is one of theorem scope and formal presentation, not a
claim to have originated balanced-ternary arithmetic rewriting.

### 8.3 Finite-state carry and normalization

Heuberger and Prodinger study carry propagation in \((q,d)\)
signed-digit expansions, explicitly identify balanced ternary as the
case \(q=3,d=-1\), and construct automata recognizing the patterns that
govern carry iterations. Finite-state descriptions of signed-digit
carry are therefore established prior art.

The theorem `add_not_DLocal` does not assert that addition is not
finite-state. It asserts only that the particular pair of quotient
states \((D(x),D(y))\) loses information needed to determine the next
quotient \(D(x+y)\). The missing finite datum is exactly the pair of
least-significant trits, or its induced carry. Adding such state is
consistent with the classical automata literature.

### 8.4 Parallel and redundant numeration systems

Frougny, Pelantová, and Svobodová develop parallel addition for broad
classes of non-standard numeration systems as a local digit-set
conversion, generalizing the Avižienis approach. Their later work gives
lower bounds and sharp constructions for minimal digit alphabets.
Frougny, Heller, Pelantová, and Svobodová compare \(k\)-block with
one-block parallel addition and show that block processing can reduce
the required alphabet.

Consequently, local normalization, finite-window arithmetic,
parallelism, and alphabet minimality are mature topics. This paper
neither proposes a parallel adder nor proves an alphabet-minimality
result. Its locality definition is the specific factorization in §4,
not the sliding-window locality used in parallel addition.

### 8.5 Relation to CMR97

The representation-level translation is exact:
\[
x :_t a\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad I_a(x),\qquad
\llbracket x :_t a\rrbracket=\llbracket I_a(x)\rrbracket=3x+a.
\]
Repeated append corresponds to composition of sections. Conversely,
every constructor-only composition of \(I_a\)'s can be written as a
CMR97 digit-append term, with the written digit order chosen to preserve
evaluation. The underlying integer representation is therefore
equivalent.

\(D\) is the inverse quotient on the image of each section:
\[
D(I_a(x))=x.
\]
The quotient interpretation is standard digit removal. What differs is
that this paper makes \(D\) primitive and studies its interaction with
append, shift, and negation as an open unary algebra. This compresses
families of digit-level equalities into operator equations, but
compression alone is not a novelty claim.

CMR97's arithmetic normalization theorems and this paper's canonical
unary theorem are not interchangeable. CMR97 is broader in arithmetic
operations; the present theorem is stronger only in its own orthogonal
direction: for the specified open unary grammar, irreducible terms are
injective under function semantics. The required orientation
\(N(D(t))\to D(N(t))\), the exact `DLocal` non-factorization theorem,
and the named push-in peak are results about this presentation. We did
not find those exact statements in the bounded audit, but make no
historical priority claim from that absence.

## 9. Scope and limitations

The exact formal scope is:

- one-hole unary terms generated by \(\{D,I_a,S,N\}\);
- integer-function semantics on \(\mathbb Z\);
- output factorization through the pair \((D(x),D(y))\);
- the finite affine constructor class in Appendix A;
- the named push-in relation in §6.

The note does not claim:

- that addition has no finite rewrite presentation;
- that arbitrary finite Add-tree systems are incomplete;
- that every extension with additional state is a computer-algebra
  system;
- that the production operator-word table is confluent;
- that AC completion is impossible or unnecessary.

Coefficient-word normalization gives a complete representation of
integer sums after evaluation. It is a different canonicalizer from
the unary `OpFrag` tree system and is not imported into the locality
proof.

## 11. Conclusion

The one-hole unary language generated by \(\{D,I_a,S,N\}\) has a
finite canonical rewrite presentation under exact integer-function
semantics. Its normal forms are unique both syntactically and as
representatives of the functions expressible in the grammar.

The next-state output of exact addition lies beyond the same local
state model:
\[
D(x+y)\ne G(D(x),D(y))
\]
for every possible \(G\). The carry identity identifies the missing
least-significant-trit information, and the named push-in system shows
the same boundary as a non-confluent critical peak. This is a precise
locality obstruction, not an impossibility theorem for arithmetic.

## Appendix A. Constructor-sum classification

Let
\[
\mathcal A=\{S,I_+,I_-,N\}.
\]
Write each constructor as \(U(t)=s_Ut+c_U\), where
\[
(s_U,c_U)\in\{(3,0),(3,1),(3,-1),(-1,0)\}.
\]
Then
\[
\forall x,y,\quad U(x)+V(y)=W(x+y)
\]
holds exactly when
\[
s_U=s_W,\qquad s_V=s_W,\qquad c_U+c_V=c_W.
\]
The Lean theorem `exactTriple_characterization` gives the eight
concrete triples
```text
(S,S,S)       (N,N,N)
(I+,S,I+)     (S,I+,I+)
(I-,S,I-)     (S,I-,I-)
(I+,I-,S)     (I-,I+,S).
```
These are six parameterized rows when the two signs are written with
\(I_a\).

In particular,
\[
I_+(x)+I_+(y)=S(x+y)+2,\qquad
I_-(x)+I_-(y)=S(x+y)-2.
\]
The constants \(\pm2\) are not balanced trits, so neither same-sign
sum is represented by a constructor in \(\mathcal A\).
