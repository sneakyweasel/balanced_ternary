# A Canonical Unary Rewrite Calculus for Balanced Ternary and the Carry Boundary

**Status:** publication draft — **READY FOR EXTERNAL REVIEW**.
Reviewer packet: [rewrite_calculus_reviewer_packet.md](rewrite_calculus_reviewer_packet.md).

## Abstract

Balanced ternary represents integers with digits in
\(\{-1,0,+1\}\). Its digit insertion and extraction maps suggest a
small local calculus generated by
\[
I_a(x)=a+3x,\qquad
D(n)=\frac{n-\operatorname{lsd}(n)}3,\qquad
S(n)=3n,\qquad N(n)=-n.
\]
We study the one-hole unary term language generated by
\(\{D,I_a,S,N\}\). Its concrete rewrite system terminates, is locally
confluent, and has a unique syntactic normal form; rewriting preserves
integer-function semantics, and distinct irreducible terms denote
distinct maps \(\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\). These results are verified in
Lean.

Exact addition crosses a precise boundary. We prove that no map
\(G:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\) satisfies
\[
D(x+y)=G(D(x),D(y))
\]
for every \(x,y\). The missing information is the carry determined by
the least-significant trits. We also classify all identities
\(U(x)+V(y)=W(x+y)\) for \(U,V,W\in\{S,I_+,I_-,N\}\), and formalize
the non-confluent critical peak of the natural carry-free push-in
extension. Thus exact addition requires state beyond the unary
\(D\)-states of its operands. This is a restricted theorem about one
concrete operator language, not an impossibility theorem for arbitrary
rewrite systems or arithmetic representations.

## 1. Introduction

Balanced ternary is a canonical signed-digit representation:
\[
n=\sum_{i\ge 0} a_i3^i,\qquad a_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}.
\]
Unique representation is classical. So are signed-digit carry
algorithms and the general methods used to prove termination and
confluence. The question here is narrower: do the natural
balanced-ternary digit operators form a closed canonical calculus, and
does that same local calculus remain adequate when exact addition is
introduced?

The answer is asymmetric:
\[
\boxed{\text{local unary digit calculus}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\text{carry-bearing arithmetic}}.
\]
Unary insertion, extraction, shift, and negation admit a complete
canonical presentation on the specified one-hole syntax. Addition does
not remain local with respect to \(D\): the pair
\((D(x),D(y))\) does not determine \(D(x+y)\). Exact arithmetic must
also retain information about the least-significant trits, equivalently
the carry they create.

The contribution is therefore not a new general rewriting theory, a
new signed-digit representation, or a claim that addition has no
finite rewrite presentation. It is a formally verified theorem package
for a concrete balanced-ternary operator language:

1. a canonical unary calculus on \(\{D,I_a,S,N\}\);
2. exact non-\(D\)-locality of addition;
3. an exhaustive classification of constructor-sum identities;
4. a concrete non-confluent peak in the natural carry-free extension.

The broader statement that every finite exact Add-tree system is
incomplete or effectively becomes coefficient-word arithmetic remains
a human-level claim. It is not a theorem of the Lean development.

## 2. The unary operator language

Let \(\operatorname{lsd}(n)\in\{-1,0,+1\}\) be the balanced residue of
\(n\) modulo \(3\). Define
\[
D(n)=\frac{n-\operatorname{lsd}(n)}3,\qquad
I_a(n)=a+3n,\qquad S(n)=3n,\qquad N(n)=-n.
\]
Here \(a\in\{-1,0,+1\}\), and \(I_0=S\) semantically.

The formal term grammar is the inductive type `OpFrag`:
```text
t ::= var | D(t) | I-(t) | I0(t) | I+(t) | S(t) | N(t).
```
It contains one variable and only unary constructors. In particular,
it contains neither `Add` nor `Mul`, and it is not the operator-word
string language used elsewhere in the project.

Evaluation assigns each term an integer function:
\[
\llbracket-\rrbracket:\operatorname{OpFrag}\to(\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z).
\]
For example,
\[
\llbracket D(t)\rrbracket(n)=D(\llbracket t\rrbracket(n)),\qquad
\llbracket I_a(t)\rrbracket(n)=I_a(\llbracket t\rrbracket(n)).
\]
This distinction matters: semantic injectivity below says that two
irreducible terms representing the same *function on every integer*
are syntactically equal. It does not say that different functions
cannot agree at an individual input.

## 3. Rewrite system and canonicality

The unary rewrite relation consists of the following root rules and
their closure under every unary context:
```text
I0(t)      → S(t)
D(I-(t))   → t
D(I0(t))   → t
D(I+(t))   → t
D(S(t))    → t
N(N(t))    → t
N(S(t))    → S(N(t))
N(I0(t))   → S(N(t))
N(I-(t))   → I+(N(t))
N(I+(t))   → I-(N(t))
N(D(t))    → D(N(t)).
```
Every rule is an exact identity of integer functions. The final
orientation is essential: without \(N(D(t))\to D(N(t))\), the terms
\(N(D(\mathrm{var}))\) and \(D(N(\mathrm{var}))\) are distinct
irreducibles with the same semantics.

### 3.1 Termination

Every rewrite step decreases the lexicographic rank
\[
(\text{number of }I_0,\ \text{\(N\)-inversions},\ \text{term size}).
\]
An \(N\)-inversion is a pair consisting of an \(N\)-node and a
pushable descendant among \(D,I_-,I_0,I_+,S\). Cancellation rules
decrease size, \(I_0\to S\) decreases the first coordinate, and rules
that move \(N\) inward decrease the second coordinate. Therefore every
rewrite order terminates.

Lean theorem: `unary_terminating`, packaging
`OpFrag.Step_terminating`.

### 3.2 Local confluence and confluence

The system is left-linear. Its root overlaps and root-versus-inner
overlaps form a finite critical-pair family, and each pair joins. This
is formalized directly for the concrete relation. Termination and
local confluence then yield confluence by the standard Newman
argument.

Lean theorem: `unary_locally_confluent`, packaging
`OpFrag.locally_confluent`; global confluence is
`OpFrag.confluent`.

### 3.3 Unique syntactic normal form

Every term rewrites to a normal term, and confluence makes that normal
term unique:
\[
\forall t\ \exists! n,\qquad t\to^* n\quad\text{and}\quad n
\text{ is irreducible}.
\]
Lean theorem: `unary_unique_nf`, packaging
`OpFrag.unique_normal_form`.

### 3.4 Semantic preservation

Every one-step rewrite preserves evaluation:
\[
t\to u\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\forall k\in\mathbb Z,\ \llbracket t\rrbracket(k)=\llbracket u\rrbracket(k).
\]
The result extends along reflexive-transitive closure. Lean theorem:
`unary_eval_step`, packaging `OpFrag.eval_step`.

### 3.5 Semantic injectivity of irreducibles

If \(t\) and \(u\) are irreducible and
\[
\forall k\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
\llbracket t\rrbracket(k)=\llbracket u\rrbracket(k),
\]
then \(t=u\). The irreducibles are therefore unique representatives
of the integer operator functions expressible in this grammar.

Lean theorem: `unary_irreducible_eval_injective`.

### 3.6 Packaged canonical-form theorem

The theorem `unary_complete_canonical_form` states that every term
\(t\) has a normal descendant \(n\), that every normal descendant of
\(t\) equals \(n\), and that every normal term semantically equal to
\(t\) also equals \(n\). “Complete” here refers exactly to this
one-hole unary grammar and exact integer-function semantics. It does
not refer to expressions containing addition or multiplication.

## 4. Relation to existing rewriting and arithmetic theory

The proof methods are standard. Newman supplies the implication from
termination and local confluence to confluence. Knuth--Bendix and
critical-pair analysis provide the general rewriting setting.
Balanced-ternary uniqueness is classical, and Avizienis established
signed-digit arithmetic and limited-carry addition as a general
arithmetic technique. Completion modulo associativity and commutativity
is also established rewriting theory.

Accordingly, this note makes no novelty claim for:

- termination or confluence theory;
- Newman or Knuth--Bendix completion;
- balanced ternary or unique signed-digit representation;
- signed-digit carries;
- AC completion.

The project-specific content is the concrete operator fragment, its
oriented \(N\)-\(D\) completion and semantic normal-form theorem, and
the exact restricted boundary encountered when `Add` is introduced.

## 5. Why addition changes the problem

Balanced-ternary addition satisfies the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
D(x+y)=D(x)+D(y)+
\operatorname{carry}(\operatorname{lsd}(x),\operatorname{lsd}(y)).
}
\]
In Lean, the carry is the second component of
`addDigit (lsdZ x) (lsdZ y)`. It belongs to
\(\{-1,0,+1\}\) and is determined by the two least-significant trits.
The theorem `carry_of_add` is a rearrangement of the existing theorem
`D_add`.

The values \(D(x)\) and \(D(y)\) retain the higher parts after the
least-significant trits have been removed. They do not retain those
removed trits. But those trits determine whether their sum produces a
carry into the higher part. Carry is therefore mathematical state, not
an implementation nuisance.

The simple equation
\[
D(x+y)=D(x)+D(y)
\]
already fails at \(x=y=1\), because \(D(2)=1\) while \(D(1)=0\).
Non-\(D\)-locality is stronger: it rules out *every* replacement
\(G(D(x),D(y))\), not only the candidate \(D(x)+D(y)\).

## 6. Exact addition is not \(D\)-local

A binary map \(F:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\) is called \(D\)-local if
there exists \(G:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\) such that
\[
D(F(x,y))=G(D(x),D(y))
\]
for all \(x,y\in\mathbb Z\).

For exact addition the formal theorem `add_not_DLocal` states
\[
\boxed{
\neg\exists G:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\quad
\forall x,y,\ D(x+y)=G(D(x),D(y)).
}
\]
The proof uses the minimal witness
\[
(x,y)=(0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(x,y)=(1,1).
\]
Indeed,
\[
D(0)=D(1)=0,
\]
so a \(D\)-local \(G\) would receive the same input \((0,0)\) in both
cases. Yet
\[
D(0+0)=0,\qquad D(1+1)=D(2)=1.
\]
No such \(G\) exists.

This theorem identifies the information loss precisely. Any exact
addition procedure at the first-digit boundary must retain information
beyond \((D(x),D(y))\), such as the least-significant trit pair or its
induced carry.

## 7. Classification of constructor-sum identities

Consider the affine unary constructors
\[
\mathcal A=\{S,I_+,I_-,N\}.
\]
Write each one as
\[
U(t)=s_Ut+c_U,
\]
where
\[
(s_U,c_U)\in\{(3,0),(3,1),(3,-1),(-1,0)\}.
\]
The predicate `exactTriple U V W` is
\[
s_U=s_W,\qquad s_V=s_W,\qquad c_U+c_V=c_W.
\]
The Lean theorem `apply_add_eq_iff` proves
\[
\forall x,y,\ U(x)+V(y)=W(x+y)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{exactTriple}(U,V,W).
\]

The theorem `exactTriple_characterization` enumerates all solutions:
```text
(S,S,S)       (N,N,N)
(I+,S,I+)     (S,I+,I+)
(I-,S,I-)     (S,I-,I-)
(I+,I-,S)     (I-,I+,S).
```
These are the eight concrete triples, or six parameterized rows when
the two signs are written using \(I_a\).

The classification shows that constructor-level closure under addition
is exceptional rather than generic. In particular,
\[
I_+(x)+I_+(y)=S(x+y)+2,\qquad
I_-(x)+I_-(y)=S(x+y)-2.
\]
The constants \(\pm2\) are not balanced trits, so no
\(W\in\mathcal A\) represents either same-sign sum. Mixed \(N\) and
\(S/I_a\) terms fail because their slopes are \(-1\) and \(3\).
These failures are formalized by `not_exact_Ip_Ip`,
`not_exact_Im_Im`, and `not_exact_N_S`.

## 8. Failure of the natural carry-free extension

To isolate a concrete rewrite obstruction, define the syntax
```text
t ::= X | Y | D(t) | S(t) | Add(t,t)
```
and the named relation generated by
```text
D(S(t))          → t
S(Add(t,u))      → Add(S(t),S(u))
```
together with congruence. There is deliberately no rule pushing
\(D\) through `Add`.

The term
\[
\boxed{D(S(\operatorname{Add}(X,Y)))}
\]
has two one-step descendants:
\[
\operatorname{Add}(X,Y)
\]
by \(D(S(t))\to t\), and
\[
D(\operatorname{Add}(S(X),S(Y)))
\]
by pushing \(S\) through `Add` inside \(D\). Both descendants are
irreducible in this named system, and they are syntactically distinct.
Nevertheless, both evaluate to \(x+y\). Thus the extension is not
locally confluent and has distinct semantic twins.

Lean theorem: `pushIn_not_locally_confluent`; semantic agreement:
`pushIn_peak_semantic`.

This is a concrete obstruction to one natural carry-free extension. It
is not a theorem about every conceivable rewrite system containing
addition.

## 9. The restricted carry-state theorem

The central formal theorem `add_requires_carry_state` is the following
conjunction:

1. addition is not \(D\)-local;
2. no constructor \(W\in\mathcal A\) represents
   \(I_+(x)+I_+(y)\);
3. no constructor \(W\in\mathcal A\) represents
   \(I_-(x)+I_-(y)\);
4. the named push-in peak rewrites to
   \(\operatorname{Add}(X,Y)\);
5. the same peak rewrites to
   \(D(\operatorname{Add}(S(X),S(Y)))\);
6. both descendants are irreducible.

In the notation of the Lean development:
```text
¬ DLocal (fun x y => x + y)
∧ (∀ W, ¬ exactTriple Ip Ip W)
∧ (∀ W, ¬ exactTriple Im Im W)
∧ PushInStep pushInPeak (Add X Y)
∧ PushInStep pushInPeak (D (Add (S X) (S Y)))
∧ irreducible (Add X Y)
∧ irreducible (D (Add (S X) (S Y))).
```

Its mathematical interpretation is:

> Under the specified unary-local semantics, exact addition cannot be
> represented using only the \(D\)-states of the operands; an exact
> extension must introduce additional carry or semantic state.

This establishes a strict informational hierarchy:
\[
\boxed{\text{unary canonical state}}
\;<\;
\boxed{\text{carry-augmented arithmetic state}}.
\]
The theorem does not specify a unique implementation of that extra
state. The pair of least-significant trits, the carry trit, or the
project's coefficient-word normal form all retain information absent
from the pair \((D(x),D(y))\).

## 11. What remains informal

The project record argues at human level that every finite exact
Add-tree extension considered is either incomplete or has introduced
the constants, carry state, AC matching, or coefficient representation
of a computer-algebra system. This broader statement appears as
`BTC-add-affine-only`, `BTC-add-factor-cas-obstruction`, and
`BTC-unary-s-distrib-obstruction`.

It is not formalized as a quantification over arbitrary term-rewriting
systems. In particular, “CAS” is not a formal mathematical predicate,
and this note does not define one. The Lean result instead fixes:

- an exact notion of \(D\)-locality;
- a finite affine constructor class;
- one named carry-free push-in relation.

The broader classification is consistent with these theorems and
motivated by them, but remains outside the scope of the formal claim.
No conjecture that addition itself is impossible is made: exact
coefficient-word normalization is an explicit counterexample to such
an overstatement.

## 12. Normalization and carry state

The unary tree calculus and coefficient-word normalization are
different canonicalizers.

The unary calculus normalizes compositions in a free one-hole tree
language. Coefficient-word normalization evaluates arithmetic
coefficients and propagates balanced carries until every coefficient
is a trit. The latter is the appropriate complete representation for
integer sums. It is already formalized separately and is not imported
into the Add-boundary proof.

This separation explains the boundary:
\[
\boxed{\text{canonical digit calculus}}
\qquad\text{versus}\qquad
\boxed{\text{normalization-aware arithmetic}}.
\]
Carry is precisely the state needed to cross from the first object to
the second. The result does not reopen the normalization branch or
claim that coefficient-word normalization is part of the unary TRS.

## 13. Minimal examples and counterexamples

### 13.1 Non-\(D\)-locality
\[
(0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(1,1)
\]
have the same operand \(D\)-states \((0,0)\), but their sums have
different \(D\)-states \(0\) and \(1\).

### 13.2 Failed naive addition rule
\[
D(1+1)=D(2)=1\ne0=D(1)+D(1).
\]

### 13.3 Natural push-in peak
\[
D(S(X+Y))
\to X+Y,\qquad
D(S(X+Y))
\to D(S(X)+S(Y)).
\]
The two descendants are irreducible in the named system and denote the
same integer function.

### 13.4 Same-sign constructor failure
\[
I_+(x)+I_+(y)=3(x+y)+2.
\]
The residue \(2\) is not in \(\{-1,0,+1\}\), so the result is not
\(I_a(x+y)\) for any balanced trit \(a\).

## 15. Conclusion

The one-hole unary language generated by
\(\{D,I_a,S,N\}\) has a closed canonical rewrite theory under exact
integer-function semantics. Exact addition does not fit the same
local state model: it is not \(D\)-local, its constructor-level
identities form a sharply limited class, and its natural carry-free
push-in extension is not locally confluent.

The resulting boundary is informational rather than absolute. Exact
addition is possible, but it requires state that remembers what the
unary \(D\)-state has discarded. In balanced ternary that state is
the least-significant trit interaction and its carry. The research
programme therefore stops at a precise theorem rather than enlarging
the rewrite table.
